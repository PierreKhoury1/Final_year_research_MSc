# Supervisor's assessment: slotbench results (RTX 3060 cloud run + A100 proof run)

## 1. Did we find anything that matters?

**Yes, but less than C1–C7 say.** One result matters in practice: MPS protects the slot from some AI neighbours and not others. One result is a solid characterisation: without isolation, misses come from the slot starting late, not running slow. One finding you have not claimed yet is real: CUDA graphs stop a slot being split mid-execution. These effects are in milliseconds, and the worst measurement error we found is about 90 µs. They show up on both GPUs and in the accidental M1/M5 repeat.

Most of the mechanism behind them is already published: the ~2 ms time slice (Gilman 2021, Bakita & Anderson RTAS'24) and stream priority not working across processes (Amert 2017). What is new is the RAN framing, the per-slot evidence and the finding that MPS protection depends on the neighbour. That is a sound MSc characterisation study. It is not a new method, it is not carrier-grade tail evidence, and it is not a consumer-vs-datacenter result.

## 3. Your actual contribution

- **Start-side misses, measured per slot, in an AI+RAN setting.** Without isolation, misses come from waiting for the GPU (latency minus exec about 2.3 ms). Under MPS that wait disappears and execution grows by 46–88% instead. This is shown without the clock mapping, on two GPUs.
- **MPS protection depends on the neighbour.** A 50% cap with a high-priority slot protects a 231 µs slot against short-unit proxy neighbours. It does not protect against a back-to-back GEMM neighbour, and that failure is not explained by clock throttling.
- **Graphs prevent mid-slot slice-out** under time-slicing, and save about 12 µs per slot.
- **An open harness** and the first AI+RAN co-location data on a GeForce card.

**Do NOT claim:**
- a new decomposition method;
- "~2 ms" as a fixed figure, or "execution unchanged";
- a start delay of 4–6 µs;
- C6's numbers;

# Adversarial verdicts

## C1 (sync examiner)
- data support: moderate; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: medium; refuted: False
- reasoning: Downgraded, not refuted. The core result holds without using the clock mapping. In the 200 worst records per cell, latency minus GPU exec is 2.25-2.29 ms for llm and sgemm under M0, M1 and M5 on the 3060, and 2.25-2.56 ms on the A100. Exec in those slots stays near idle, so these misses are start-side. MPS removes the effect (latency minus exec 8-14 us), which is good indirect evidence for a context-level cause. The d100 miss rates are 95-100% and the d50 rates are 50-64%, as claimed. Three sub-claims are wrong or overstated.
(1) 'Execution unchanged (~231-234 us)' is false for sgemm. On the 3060, exec p50 is 261-262 us against 231.4 us idle (+13%), and 274 us in M4. Telemetry shows SwPowerCap in 100% of samples at 169.7/170 W, with the SM clock at 1717-1725 MHz against 1957 MHz; the clock ratio of 1.134 matches the exec ratio of 1.128. On the A100, idle exec is 220 us, not 231 (4 vs 5 LDPC iterations), and sgemm raises it 5% under thermal slowdown.
(2) '~2 ms' is not universal. The vision neighbour gives 1.18 ms p50 and 1.45 ms max, bounded by the adversary's per-frame cudaStreamSynchronize rather than by a slice. The ~2 ms figure for llm and sgemm is also partly set by the closed-loop driver: one slot in flight, next launch at most 500 us after the GPU returns to the AI context, so latency falls in a band from T-500+exec to T+exec.
(3) The 'start late' mechanism is specific to graph mode. In streams mode, which is still no isolation, 152 of the 200 worst M4 llm d50 slots have exec of about 2.50 ms: the slot was sliced out mid-execution. So without isolation, misses can also look like 'ran slow'.
Other points: 'GPU time-slicing' is inferred, not tested here (M3 not run, no slice microbenchmark), though it is consistent with Gilman 2021 (~2 ms fixed slice, RTX 3090) and Bakita & Anderson RTAS'24 (~2 ms, GTX 1060). The d50 miss rates just track the adversary's active fraction (sgemm d50 is really 62-64% active). Skipped boundaries inflate miss rates: on the A100 llm M0, 80.8% of processed slots were late, but the miss rate is 95%. 'Same on A100' holds only in shape: the A100 idle baseline itself misses 1.12% (all host-side), and the A100 cells are d100 only, with no vision. Novelty is strict. YinYangRAN (INFOCOM'24, A100) already shows naive GPU sharing gives about 50% deadline compliance and names time-sliced context switching as the cause. The ~2 ms slice and the 'never concurrent across processes' behaviour are documented by Gilman, Bakita, GCAPS and NVIDIA's MPS docs. Only the per-slot RAN measurement that the delay is start-side, about one slice, is new. Practically it matters as a motivating baseline and as a warning for Kubernetes time-slicing. Nobody deploys this configuration deliberately.
- threats: Closed-loop, one-in-flight driver shapes the delay distribution and inflates misses through skipped boundaries. Per-unit sync in the adversary bounds the delay for short units. No RT kernel and no SCHED_FIFO: timer slack is ~54 us, and A100 wake overshoot reaches 5 ms. No clock locking, so power-cap and thermal throttling confound exec. One run per cell on one rented instance per GPU. LDPC iterations, drivers and hosts differ between the GPUs. Time-slicing as the cause is not directly manipulated. Queue-delay values depend on a clock mapping with up to ~18 us (3060) and ~88 us (A100) of in-run error; this is irrelevant at 2 ms but matters for any sub-100 us statement. The A100 idle baseline fails the project's own validator.
- to strengthen: Reword per workload and per launch mode: 'start delay up to one time slice (~2.3 ms) or the neighbour's sync interval, whichever is shorter. Exec is within ~3% for llm and vision but +13% (3060) and +5% (A100) under sgemm, from power and thermal throttling. In streams mode the slot can also be sliced mid-execution.' Make latency minus exec the primary, mapping-free metric. Add an open-loop driver mode with random launch phase. Add a direct time-slice microbenchmark (two processes running persistent kernels that log %globaltimer), and run M3 on a host with root. Sweep the adversary unit length. Report the power-cap and throttle state per cell. Repeat runs on different instances, and split host-side misses out of the miss rate.

## C6 (sync examiner)
- data support: contradicted; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: low; refuted: True
- reasoning: Refuted as stated. I recomputed from clock_fits in all 44 summary.json files.
(1) The ±1.2 us bound holds only on the 3060, and only inside the ~0.14 s pre and post calibration windows (eps 1.12-1.33 us). On the A100, eps is 2.19-2.77 us, so 'on NVIDIA' is false. The bound is essentially the %globaltimer tick, not a property of the clocks. All 8,800 worst-record exec values are exact multiples of 1024 ns, and the fit residual rms of 290-360 ns is about 1.024/sqrt(12) us.
(2) Drift < ~1.6 ppm is contradicted. Single-window rate fits range from -8.5 to +12.0 ppm, and pre vs post differs by up to ~10 ppm (3060 M2 vision d50: +5.12 vs -4.82). Run-average two-point rates are -0.90 to -3.00 ppm on the 3060 and depend on load and temperature (about -1.0 idle and llm vs -2.1 to -3.0 under sgemm and vision d100). On the A100 they are +7.4 to +9.3 ppm. The 1.57 ppm figure is just the A100 median of |pre - post|.
(3) In-run agreement is far worse than the claim, and the data prove it with physically impossible values. Queue delay goes down to -17.8 us (3060 M1 sgemm d100) and -88.3 us (A100 M0 sgemm), and three 3060 sgemm d50 cells have a negative median. The host-detection lag t1 - host(g1) goes down to -22.6 us (A100 M1 llm), meaning the host 'saw' completion before the GPU finished. On 3060 M1 sgemm d100 the lag bows from 4-7 us at the ends to 20-24 us mid-run, which is non-linear thermal drift hidden by straight-line interpolation between two anchors 51-255 s apart. Realistic in-run agreement is about ±20 us on hot 3060 runs and about ±90 us on hot A100 runs: 15-70x worse than claimed.
The failure does not threaten the ms-scale C1 result, because exec (g1 - g0) uses one clock. It does undermine the '4-6 us start delay under MPS' claim (C3) and the start-side half of C7 for near-deadline misses. Novelty: CPU-GPU timestamp correlation is standard (CUPTI and Nsight), and the globaltimer resolution is documented. This is a method-validation figure, not a research result, and 'new for RAN' carries no weight here.
- threats: Two-point linear interpolation assumes a constant rate, but the data show a temperature- and load-dependent rate. Calibration windows are short (~0.14 s), so per-window slopes are poorly determined. Clock resolution is 1.024 us. The A100 host has noisy CPU scheduling, which widens the brackets. No long-duration clockcal data were shipped. The globaltimer is initialised from host realtime, while the host reference is CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW, and possible driver adjustments to the GPU timer were not ruled out.
- to strengthen: Run bin/clockcal for hours, idle and under each load, logging GPU temperature and SM clock, and report rate vs temperature. Recalibrate during the run, or track the offset with a moving-window minimum of (t1 - g1), which bounds completion-detection lag from below. Add a validation failure for negative queue delay or negative detection lag. Restate C6 as: 'at calibration, ±1.3 us (3060) and ±2.8 us (A100), limited by the 1.024 us timer tick; rate offsets of -1 to -3 ppm (3060, load-dependent) and +7 to +9 ppm (A100); in-run mapping error up to ~20/90 us'. Report start-side numbers as latency minus exec wherever possible.

## C7 (sync examiner)
- data support: weak; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: medium; refuted: False
- reasoning: Heavily downgraded. Each of the three parts is weaker than claimed.
(a) 'Decomposing each miss into started-late vs ran-slow.'
- Not delivered as stated. The analysis code (stats.py, summarize.py, report.py) outputs only separate marginal distributions of queue_delay and gpu_exec. There is no per-miss attribution. Raw slots.bin were not shipped (only the 200 worst records per cell), so a per-miss split cannot be reproduced for most misses.
- The split is incomplete. A third, host-side cause (late wake, or late detection of completion) is missing. It explains all 200 worst late slots in the A100 M0 idle cell (exec 220 us, start on time, latency minus exec > 250 us) and about 36 of 87 late slots in A100 MPS llm.
- It mislabels. Streams-mode mid-slot preemption (M4 llm d50: 152/200 worst slots with exec ≈ 2.5 ms) and, under MPS, waiting for SMs after the 1-thread start-stamp kernel both get filed as 'ran slow'. Throttling and contention are not separated either.
- The start component depends on a clock mapping with ≥18 us (3060) and ≥88 us (A100) in-run error (see C6). That error matters exactly for the near-deadline MPS misses where attribution is interesting.
- What does stand is a mapping-free, distribution-level demonstration: worst-slot latency minus exec is ~2.3 ms without isolation and ~10 us under MPS.
(b) Novelty. Separating launch-to-start latency from kernel execution is standard: Nsight's launch-latency definition, CUPTI queued/submitted/start/end records, NVIDIA HoloHub's green-context launch-start benchmark under background load, and the 2026 eunomia.dev 'slow or started late' framing. In RAN, CloudRIC splits waiting time from processing time. Weaver (arXiv 2609.35276) reports an 'isolation violation' rate, 'when a training kernel still occupies SMs required by the RAN at decoding start' (2.7% → 0.6%), which is a start-side interference metric. The statement that Weaver does not report this is therefore only narrowly true: Weaver gives no per-miss latency split, but it does measure start-side interference. YinYangRAN already names time-sliced context switching as the cause in RAN. Only YinYangRAN was read in full by the student (related_work.md).
(c) 'Worst-case tails over many slots' is unsupported. Each cell has 100k slots (DESIGN asked for 1e6 × 3 repeats), from one run on one instance. p99.99 is the 10th-largest sample: its 95% range on A100 M2 llm is 545-2607 us. The max is a single slot. Zero-miss cells only bound the miss rate below 3.7e-5, far from the 1e-5 to 1e-6 framing of Concordia, Nuberu and CloudRIC. Misses cluster, so the Clopper-Pearson CIs are too narrow: the M1 and M5 replicate CIs do not overlap.
(d) The consumer-vs-datacenter comparison is confounded. LDPC iterations differ (5 vs 4), as do drivers, host CPUs and the slot's share of the SMs (22/28 vs 22/108 blocks). The A100 adversary throughput is diluted unevenly by its settle period. There is one instance per GPU, and the A100 has only 9 cells and a failing idle baseline. It is apparently the first AI+RAN co-location data on a consumer GPU (Barker 2026 is LDPC only), but GPU class is not the isolated variable.
Why it still matters: Aerial's real failure modes (order-kernel and HARQ-wait timeouts) are start-side, so a start-vs-exec diagnostic is useful to practitioners. However, the harness's 'CPU launches at the boundary' model does not match Aerial's data-driven pipeline.
- threats: Two-stamp design (1-thread start stamp, end stamp) cannot see SM availability or preemption inside the slot. Start-side values depend on a drifting clock mapping. Only the worst-200 records are available per cell. The closed-loop driver and skipped-boundary rule mix CPU-side and GPU-side misses. Noisy cloud hosts with no RT kernel add host-side tails, especially on the A100. Single runs and single instances. Adversaries are proxies, and the real-LLM cells all failed. The literature sweep was largely unverified when C7 was written.
- to strengthen: Implement and ship a real per-slot attribution with at least four classes: CPU wake or launch late, GPU start late, GPU exec long, and host detection late. Use mapping-free latency minus exec plus start_lateness and launch_cost, and report the class shares per cell. Add timestamps at each stage (around LDPC and the big kernels) so that SM-wait under MPS and mid-slot preemption are not counted as 'ran slow'. Ship slots.bin. Run ≥1e6 slots per cell with ≥3 repeats on different instances, and use cluster-aware CIs such as a block bootstrap. Equalise slot work and SM fraction across GPUs (same LDPC iterations and cell count) before claiming a consumer-vs-datacenter difference, or reframe it as GDDR/no-MIG vs HBM/MIG. Cite Weaver's isolation-violation metric, CloudRIC, HoloHub and CUPTI, and position the contribution as RAN-specific attribution rather than a new method.

## C2 (mechanisms examiner)
- data support: strong; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: low; refuted: False
- reasoning: The measurement holds. On the 3060, M0 and M1 differ in miss rate by -0.22 to +0.12 pp across all six contended cells (for example llm d50 54.03 vs 54.02, vision d50 50.49 vs 50.40, sgemm d50 63.03 vs 63.25). That is the same size as the accidental M1/M5 replicate spread (up to 0.10 pp). Queue p50 (1916-1999 us at d100) and exec p50 also match to within 1-2 us. On the A100, llm gives 94.98 vs 95.11%. The test is fair: meta.json shows the driver stream at -5 (greatest) and the adversary at 0. On this device 0 is both 'low' and default, so M1 spans the full priority range. I am downgrading it on three grounds. (1) It is not a finding. Amert et al. (RTSS 2017) state 'assigning priorities to the streams of different processes had no effect', and GCAPS (ECRTS 2024) and Gilman 2021 say the same. It follows from documented per-context (TSG) time-slicing, so it is a positive confirmation of documented behaviour, new only in a RAN setting. (2) Only some cells can test it. The d100 cells sit at 99.9-100% misses on the 3060, so M0 and M1 cannot differ there. Only the three d50 cells and the A100 llm cell carry information. (3) 'Across processes' is worded too broadly. The NVIDIA MPS documentation says the driver does honour stream priorities across MPS clients, and the student's own M2 runs the slot at high priority under MPS. The claim is only shown for time-sliced contexts without MPS. Under MPS, priority may be doing real work, and that was never isolated (see C3).
- threats: Scope: tested only under time-slicing, not under MPS, where cross-client priority is documented to apply. Ceiling effect: d100 cells are saturated near 100% misses. Single run per cell, so the CIs understate clustered error. One driver version per GPU, on the closed-source scheduler. Ampere only. There is no positive control showing that priority works inside one process in this harness, so a harness bug in applying priority cannot be fully excluded; meta.json stream_reported=-5 makes this unlikely.
- to strengthen: Reword to 'stream priority has no effect between time-sliced processes (no MPS)'. Add a positive control in which the adversary runs as a low-priority stream inside the driver process, showing priority works there. Add M2 with the driver at default priority, plus CUDA_MPS_CLIENT_PRIORITY, to show where cross-process priority does act. Cite Amert 2017 and GCAPS as the known result and present this as a confirmation in the RAN setting.

## C3 (mechanisms examiner)
- data support: moderate; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: high; refuted: False
- reasoning: The core effect is large and appears on both GPUs. Under M2, the 3060 queue delay drops from about 1.9-2.0 ms to idle level (mapping-free latency minus exec is 8-14 us on the worst slots). Misses are 0-0.005% against the llm and vision proxies, versus 15.0% (d50) and 32.8% (d100) against sgemm. The A100 shows sgemm at 18.4%. Telemetry shows the 3060 M2 sgemm SM clock only 7% down (1830 vs 1957 MHz) and the A100 M2 sgemm unthrottled at 1410 MHz, so the sgemm failure is contention, not just power capping. Much of the claim's wording does not survive scrutiny, though. (1) 'Start delay ~4-6 us' is below what the clock mapping resolves. Queue delays go negative (-17.9 us on the 3060, -88 us on the A100), and M2 vision and sgemm medians (1.4 and 2.1 us) sit below idle. (2) 'Restores start sync' is partly true by construction. S0 is a 1-thread stamp kernel (phy_kernels.cu:172) that fits on any free SM, so later kernels waiting for SMs held by sgemm blocks are counted as 'execution'. The started-late vs ran-slow split cannot separate SM queueing from slow running under MPS. (3) M2 is confounded. It turns on MPS, caps only the adversary at 50% (the driver is uncapped) and keeps the slot at high priority (run_one.sh:95,109). MPS honours cross-client priority, so the protection may come from MPS plus priority-ordered block dispatch, not MPS alone. (4) 'Shared SMs' is asserted, not shown. On the A100 the 50% cap leaves 54 SMs free for 22 LDPC blocks, yet exec p99 reaches 792 us, so SM count is not the explanation. M2 idle already costs +7% (247.8 vs 231.4 us). (5) 'Protects against LLM/vision' depends on headroom. The slot is 231 us idle and inflates about 1.5x to 389-396 us exec p99.99, so a slot above about 320 us idle would fail. Zero-miss cells only bound the rate below 3.7e-5 (100k slots, one run), far from 1e-5. The 3060 M2 llm d50 cell has a 557 us start delay, so start sync is not always restored. (6) 'LLM vs GEMM' is a label on proxies. The LLM proxy is a GEMV chain that synchronises after every token (adversary.cu:673-679), giving short-lived blocks and gaps. The sgemm unit is one 4096^3 kernel of about 33 ms with long-resident blocks. The data therefore support 'short-block neighbours vs long-resident-block neighbours', which is the behaviour the CUDA green-context documentation already describes ('the critical kernel can start executing as soon as some thread blocks complete'). Batched LLM serving or prefill would behave like GEMM. The real-LLM cells all failed. (7) About half of each sgemm miss figure is skipped boundaries from the one-slot-in-flight closed loop. Per processed slot, 7.9% (d50) and 19.4% (d100) were late. The 3060 maximum latency is 677 us, so an overlapping 1200 us PUSCH budget would give about 0 misses. (8) The A100's 0.4% against llm is mostly host noise: of 87 late slots, about 36 are late completion detection and only 48 are exec over 450 us. Novelty: YinYangRAN and Aerial ISAC already show that MPS protects the RAN at an AI or memory-contention cost. Neighbour-dependent MPS interference is known in general GPU work (Gilman 2021, Villarrubia 2026, the green-context documentation). It is new only in the RAN framing. It matters because MPS is the only spatial option on GPUs without MIG or with 2-way MIG (L40S, DGX Spark, ARC-Pro), and training or GEMM work is what the spare capacity would be sold for.
- threats: (a) MPS, priority and the adversary-only 50% cap are confounded; no cap sweep, no M2 at default priority, no driver cap, no client priority. (b) The 1-thread start stamp makes 'start on time' partly a construction artefact, and SM-wait is counted as exec. (c) Clock-mapping error of 10-90 us during a run makes the 4-6 us figure meaningless. (d) A single slot size with about 2x deadline headroom drives the 'protects' verdict. (e) Proxies only, with per-unit sync in the adversary, so the class labels LLM, vision and GEMM do not transfer. (f) 100k slots and one run per cell; p99.99 rests on about 10 slots; zero misses only gives an upper bound of 3.7e-5. (g) The closed-loop one-in-flight policy doubles the sgemm miss counts through skipped boundaries. (h) The A100 host was noisy (wake overshoot up to 5 ms), which contaminates the 0.4%. (i) Cloud containers had no SCHED_FIFO, no clock lock, and the 3060 hit its power cap in the sgemm and vision cells.
- to strengthen: Run an MPS factorial: no cap and caps of 25/50/75%, driver default vs high priority, a cap on the driver as well, and CUDA_MPS_CLIENT_PRIORITY. Add per-stage timestamps, at least around the LDPC decode, so SM-wait is separated from slow execution. Report 'latency minus exec within ~10 us of idle' instead of 4-6 us. Sweep slot size (--tune-us 150-350) and report the largest slot MPS still protects. Sweep adversary kernel or block duration (sgemm --size, a batched GEMV and prefill-like LLM, a real llama.cpp or vLLM, YOLO) to show the dependence is on block residency, not on the workload label. ## C4 (mechanisms examiner)
- data support: contradicted; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: low; refuted: True
- reasoning: 'M4 == M1' is contradicted by the data in three ways. (1) A fixed cost: streams mode adds about 12 us (+5%) of exec in every cell, contended or not (idle 243.7 vs 231.4 us, llm d100 246.8 vs 234.5, sgemm d100 274.4 vs 262.1). Launch cost p50 is 40.5 us vs 2.2 us. The idle p99.99 latency is 282.6 vs 251.1 us. (2) A new failure mode that graphs remove: in streams mode a slot can start on time and then be sliced out mid-execution. I checked the worst-200 records of M4 llm d50. In 152 of them, exec is 2498-2504 us (one full time slice) and latency minus exec is only about 8 us, so these slots started on time and were preempted. M4 sgemm d50 has exec p99.9 of 2523 us, and sgemm d100 has a maximum of 2556 us. Across the 21 contended graph-mode cells (M0, M1, M5), exec never exceeds 268 us. The cause is the documented work-conserving scheduler behaviour: when host-side enqueuing (about 40 us) lets the slot's queue run dry, the scheduler yields to the adversary. A graph submits the whole slot at once, so the queue never runs dry. (3) Miss rates differ by more than replicate noise where the cells are not saturated. llm d50: 54.48% vs 54.02% (M1) and 54.06% (M5). sgemm d50: 63.92% vs 63.25% and 63.33%. The M1/M5 spread is 0.04-0.08 pp. The extra late slots (+161 for llm, +136 to +204 for sgemm) match the number of split slots. Vision d50 shows no difference (50.51 vs 50.40 and 50.50), and the d100 cells sit at 99.9-100% for both modes. That apparent equality is a ceiling effect, not evidence of no difference. The defensible statement is the opposite of the claim: under time-slicing, graphs protect a slot from being split mid-execution and save about 12 us per slot. The practical size under M0 and M1 is small, because both modes already miss 50-100%. The test that would matter, M4 under MPS where slots actually meet the deadline, was not run. Novelty: no paper tests graphs vs streams under cross-process contention. However, the per-TSG work-conserving time-slicing that predicts both the equal start delay and the mid-slot split is published (Bakita and Anderson RTAS 2024, Olmedo RTAS 2020), and the launch-overhead saving of graphs is documented by NVIDIA (cuPHY, RANPerf).
- threats: Single run per cell, and the Clopper-Pearson CIs are too narrow because misses cluster (one split slot causes several skipped boundaries). The replicate noise estimate rests on one M1/M5 pair. Split detection uses only the 200 worst records plus quantiles, because slots.bin was not shipped. M4 was run only as 'M1 + streams', not under MPS or M0. The split rate depends on host enqueue speed in a container without SCHED_FIFO, so it is machine-specific. The current breakdown misclassifies these splits as 'GPU ran slow' rather than as a scheduling delay.
- to strengthen: Withdraw 'M4 == M1'. Restate as 'graph launch removes the ~12 us enqueue cost and prevents mid-slot slice-out; start delay under time-slicing is unchanged'. Ship slots.bin or a per-slot exec histogram and count the slices directly. Run M4 under MPS and at several duty values with repeats. Measure host enqueue gaps (for example with CUPTI or nsys) to confirm the yield-on-empty-queue mechanism. Add a third breakdown category ('preempted mid-slot') to the analysis.

- data support: weak; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: low; refuted: True

(2) 94% vs 64% is arithmetically right but not what it is framed as. Recomputed from adversary.json: A100 410.5/434.8 = 0.944; 3060 86.4/134.2 = 0.644. ## C7 (generality examiner)
- data support: weak; novelty: known_generally_new_for_ran; matters: low; refuted: True
- reasoning: Of the three parts of this contribution claim, the second (tails) is contradicted by the sample sizes and the third (consumer vs datacenter) is confounded (see C5). The first, the two-way decomposition, survives only in a narrower form.

(1) The project does not decompose 'each miss'.
- analysis/stats.py and summarize.py compute only marginal distributions of queue_delay, gpu_exec and wake. There is no per-miss attribution, and raw slots.bin was not shipped.
- Per-miss attribution is possible only for the 200 worst records per cell. Cells with about 20,000-100,000 late slots therefore cannot be decomposed slot by slot.
- Skipped boundaries have no GPU stamps at all, yet they are about half of the MPS-sgemm misses (20,024 of 39,392 on the 3060; 10,330 of 20,351 on the A100) and 310 of 397 miss events in A100 M2 llm. They cannot be decomposed.

(2) The two-way split leaves out a third cause: the host (CPU wake-up, launch, noticing completion late). This cause explains every A100 idle miss and about three-quarters of the A100 MPS-llm miss rate (about 0.3 of 0.4 pp). There are also two definitions of 'late' (stats.py:112-114): measured from the slot boundary, A100 M2 llm has 199 late slots, not 87.

(3) The 'ran slow' bin is contaminated by start-side effects.
- The start stamp is a 1-thread kernel (phy_kernels.cu:172), so under MPS the time spent waiting for SMs counts as execution.
- In streams mode, being sliced out mid-slot counts as execution: exec reaches 2.5 ms in M4.

(4) 'Started late' is resolved only at the millisecond scale.
- The in-run clock-mapping error is about 10-20 us on the 3060 and up to about 88 us on the A100, as shown by queue delays that go physically negative.
- So the 4-6 us 'restored sync' under MPS is below resolution.
- The robust result, that latency minus exec is about 2.25 ms under time-slicing, is what the documented ~2 ms time slice predicts (Gilman 2021; Bakita & Anderson RTAS'24; NVIDIA MPS docs).

(5) Prior art.
- Separating launch-to-start from start-to-end is standard: Nsight and CUPTI record queued/submitted/start/end; cuda_scheduling_examiner; NVIDIA's HoloHub green-context benchmark measures launch-start time under background load; eunomia.dev's 'slow or started late?'.
- RAN analogues exist: CloudRIC splits waiting from processing, and Weaver counts 'isolation violations' when training kernels still hold the SMs at decoding start. 'Weaver does not report' is therefore only partly true.
- related_work.md admits only YinYangRAN was read in full.

(6) 'Worst-case tails over many slots' is not delivered.
- Each cell has 100k slots (about 50 s) and was run once, against the ~1e6 slots and 3 repeats in DESIGN.md.
- p99.99 is the 10th-largest sample. The A100 M2 llm p99.99 has a 95% order-statistic range of 545-2607 us.
- Zero-miss cells only bound the miss rate below 3.7e-5, short of the 99.999% framing already set by Concordia, Nuberu and CloudRIC.

(7) Industry fit.
- The 'CPU asks, GPU starts' notion of sync does not describe Aerial, whose uplink is data-driven: a pre-launched order kernel polls the NIC, graphs launch on the device, and threads run SCHED_FIFO.
- Deployments use MIG or MPS, where start delay is about zero and the residual risk is execution inflation or the host. A two-way split adds little there.

What survives: a mapping-free split of slot latency into 'waited for the GPU' vs 'executed', measured in an AI+RAN setting with a separate-process AI co-tenant. It shows time-slicing failures are start-side and that MPS turns them into execution inflation. This is new for RAN but a modest, method-level contribution.
- threats: - No per-miss attribution in the pipeline: only marginals plus the worst 200 records; skipped boundaries cannot be decomposed.
- The host-side third cause is missing from the two-way split.
- The 1-thread start stamp hides SM-wait under MPS; mid-slot preemption in streams mode is booked as execution.
- Clock-mapping drift within a run (10-90 us) limits start attribution to the millisecond scale.
- 100k slots, one run per cell; p99.99 rests on about 10 slots; the A100 tails are host artefacts.
- 'Sync' is defined by CPU launch, which does not match deployed data-driven L1 pipelines.
- Proxy AI workloads only.
- The prior-art check is incomplete (only YinYangRAN read in full).
- to strengthen: - Implement per-slot, per-miss attribution with at least three causes (host wake/launch/detection, GPU start delay, GPU execution) in summarize.py, using the mapping-free latency minus exec plus the host stamps. Assign skipped boundaries to their cause, and ship slots.bin or per-cause counts.
- Add timestamp kernels around the LDPC stage, or per-stage stamps, so SM-wait under MPS and mid-slot preemption are separated from true slow execution.
- Flag negative queue delays, and recalibrate the clocks during the run.
- Run at least 1e6 slots per cell with at least 3 repeats on different instances, and report consecutive-miss bursts.
- Test the split on a data-driven trigger (a persistent polling kernel like Aerial's order kernel) and under MIG and MPS with a capped RAN.
- Read Weaver, Aerial ISAC and CloudRIC in full, and position the work against Weaver's isolation-violation metric and CUPTI/Nsight launch-latency tooling. Claim it as an application of a known decomposition to AI-RAN, not as a new method.
- Drop 'consumer vs datacenter' from the contribution until C5 is de-confounded.

## 2. Integrity checks that passed
- **Counts are clean.** All 35 cells on the 3060 and all 9 valid A100 cells recorded exactly 100,000 of 100,000 requested slots. Ring overflows, stamp mismatches and missing stamps are all 0, and every run exited "completed". Every queue delay uses the `two_point` method, both clock fits are ok, and each fit kept 201–1225 samples.
- **Miss rates and CIs match.** Miss rates and Clopper-Pearson CIs recompute exactly in all 44 cells.
- **Latency quantiles match.** All 132 latency quantiles (p50, p99, p99.99 and max) agree with the histogram bins and the worst-200 list, with no mismatches.
- **Skipped-boundary counts differ only at the end.** `meta.counts.skipped_boundaries` is 0–5 higher than the summary, because it includes boundaries after the last record. That only triggers the `skipped_consistent` warning.
- **The adversary was running for the whole driver window.**
  - It started 10.0 s (3060) or 30.0 s (A100) before the driver and ended 0.09–0.28 s after it.
  - `adversary_alive_at_driver_end` is 1, and units > 0 in every non-idle cell.
  - Telemetry utilisation inside the driver window has a median of 98–100% at d100 and 73–84% at d50. Idle cells sit at 45–51%, which is the slot alone.
- **MPS was really on in every M2 cell.**
  - `run.json` `mps_status` lists the control daemon and one server PID; a server only exists once a client has connected, and the status was taken 10 s after the adversary started.
  - `adversary.json` shows `mps_active_thread_percentage` = "50", and the driver ran with the MPS pipe directory in its environment.
  - Behaviourally, the start delay fell from about 2 ms to idle level while utilisation stayed at 99–100%, which time-slicing cannot produce.
- **Stream priorities were really set.**
  - In `meta.stream_priority`, value and stream_reported are -5 (the greatest priority) for M1, M2, M4 and M5, and 0 for M0.
  - The adversary's `prio_value` is 0 everywhere. The device's range runs from 0 (least) to -5 (greatest), so the adversary's "low" is the same as default. M1 is therefore "driver high vs adversary default".
- **M5 is an exact repeat of M1.** Clock locking and unlocking were refused in all 38 `run.json` files (`NOT SUPPORTED`, rc=3). That gives a free replicate run for every M1 cell.

## 4. Anomalies and data-quality caveats
- **CPU timer slack.** Wake overshoot p50 is 53.7–56.4 µs in every cell. That matches Linux's default 50 µs timer slack (no SCHED_FIFO in a container), so the planned 50 µs spin never happens. Start lateness p50 is 3.8–6.5 µs.
  - The largest wake overshoot is 1.72 ms on the 3060 and 5.01 ms on the A100 (M2 llm), not 4.6 ms as stated.
- **Idle-cell misses on the 3060 are CPU or launch effects, not GPU sharing.**
  - M0 idle has 0 late slots and 6 skipped; one CPU stall (start lateness 1671 µs) caused 3 of the skips.
  - M4 idle's 3 late slots: 2 were the host noticing late (tail 336–401 µs) and 1 was a 596 µs exec caused by an enqueue stall in streams mode.
- **The A100 idle baseline fails the project's own check.** M0 idle has a 1.12% miss rate, p99.99 1227 µs and maximum 9.47 ms. All of its late slots are the host noticing late: GPU exec 220 µs, with the host seeing completion 0.28–10 ms afterwards.
  - The A100 also has rare exec stalls of about +150–200 µs even when idle (exec p99.99 406.5 µs). The 3060 does not.
- **The project's own validator would reject many cells.** It fails on throttling in 18 of the 3060 cells (sgemm and vision) and in A100 M0/M1 sgemm. It fails the idle-baseline check in M0, M1, M2 and M4 idle on the 3060 and in all three A100 idle cells. The report includes these cells anyway.
- **Relative throughput is missing from the summaries.** `adversary_relative_throughput` is null in every `summary.json`, because the SOLO folders lack the `_r1` suffix the code looks for. All relative numbers have to be computed by hand; I recomputed them above.
- **Real-LLM cells are empty.** All real-LLM cells and `SOLO_real-llm` have status `invalid:warmup_adversary_rc1` and contain no data.

## 5. How far the sample sizes go (100k slots per cell, one run each)
- **p99.99 rests on about 10 slots.** It is the 10th-largest value; the 95% order-statistic range runs from the 4th to the 17th largest.
  - On the 3060 that is tight: M2 llm d100 is 398.7 µs, range 397.9–401.2.
  - On the A100 it is meaningless: M2 llm 1976 µs, range 545–2607 µs.
- **p99.999 and the maximum are single slots.** In the M1 vs M5 repeat, the maximum varies: 256 against 307 µs idle, 2501 against 2758 µs for llm d100.
- **Zero-miss cells only prove misses < 3.69e-5.** Proving a rate below 1e-5 would need at least about 3e5 clean slots.

## Audit: method

# Methodology review of slotbench claims C1–C7

## Did we do anything that matters?

Yes, but less than C1–C7 currently claim.

**What holds up.** When the AI job runs in a separate process with no isolation, slots miss because they wait for the GPU, not because they run slowly. MPS removes that wait. Three things support this:
- It shows up without the clock mapping. Across the 200 worst slots per cell, latency minus GPU execution time is 2.25–2.29 ms for M0/M1 against llm and sgemm, 1.44–1.46 ms against vision, and 8–14 µs under M2.
- M5 turned out to be an accidental repeat of M1, and the two agree: miss rates differ by at most 0.1 percentage points (pp).
- The A100 shows the same shape.

**What does not hold up as stated:** the fixed "~2 ms", "execution unchanged", "start delay 4–6 µs", "±1.2 µs / <1.6 ppm", "M4 == M1" and the 94% vs 64% comparison.

**Only one isolation option really separated anything.** That was MPS, at a single 50% setting, and it is mixed with other settings (see C3). M1 and M4 sit at ~100% misses, so nothing can differ. M5 is a duplicate of M1. M3, M6 and green contexts were not run.

**Default time slice (you asked).** NVIDIA does not publish the length. `nvidia-smi compute-policy` only offers DEFAULT/SHORT/MEDIUM/LONG. NVIDIA's open driver source says "exact timeslice values can be chosen appropriately as per GPU support" (`ctrl2080gpu.h`). It sets a per-process default of 128<<3 = 1024 µs (`kernel_fifo_gv100.c:51`), which the closed CUDA driver can override per process. Bakita & Anderson (RTAS'24) measured slices "at about 2 ms each" on a GTX 1060, and 1024 µs on a Jetson TX2. Our longest start delays are 2.26–2.29 ms on the 3060 and 2.59–2.67 ms on the A100. That fits roughly a 2 ms slice plus the cost of switching processes. M3 never ran, so we have not shown cause and effect.

## C1: co-located AI makes slots start ~2 ms late; execution unchanged; same on both GPUs

- **[H] "Execution unchanged" is false for sgemm.**
  - 3060 GPU execution time (median): 231.4 µs idle vs 261.1 µs (M0) and 262.1 µs (M1) with sgemm at 100% duty, i.e. +13%.
  - A100: 220.2 vs 231.4 µs (+5%).
  - Cause: power-cap throttling. The 3060 telemetry shows 170 W (the card's limit), the power-cap flag (0x4) in 259 of 259 samples, and a median SM clock of 1725 vs 1957 MHz. The A100 drops from 1410 to 1365 MHz.
  - This is a shared-resource effect (power and heat) that no isolation mechanism removes. Report it; don't hide it.
  - The claim needs rewording per workload; the start-delay part still stands.
- **[M/H] "~2 ms" partly comes from how we built the driver and the AI job.**
  - The driver never queues slots. After a slot finishes, the GPU goes back to the AI process, and the next launch comes at most 500 µs later, so it waits most of a full slice. Latency therefore sits in a band [T − 500 + exec, T + exec], with a maximum of about 2.50–2.60 ms. With real slots arriving every 500 µs regardless, waits would spread from 0 to T.
  - The AI job synchronises after every unit (`adversary.cu:673-679`, blocking sync at `:605`). That leaves idle gaps where the slot gets in, so the delay is capped by the unit length:
    - vision (frames ~1.4 ms): all slots fall between 1.0 and 1.75 ms; start delay median 1179 µs, max 1449 µs.
    - llm on the 3060: 23% of slots at 1.0–1.5 ms and 75% at 2.0–2.5 ms.
    - llm on the A100 (~3 ms tokens): 40% of slots at 0.3–1.0 ms.
  - Restate as "up to one time slice (~2.3 ms), set by the slice or the AI job's sync interval, whichever is shorter".
  - The 50%-duty miss rates (50–63%) mostly just reflect that the AI job is on half of each 100 ms period.
  - **Fixes:**
    - an open-loop mode that launches every boundary, plus a random phase;
    - sweep the AI unit length (sgemm `--size`, LLM with and without CUDA graphs);
    - a direct slice measurement: two processes each running a persistent kernel that logs the GPU timer;
    - M3 on a host where we have root (home lab).
- **[L] Clock-mapping error** (see C6) is small next to 2 ms. "Same on A100" holds in shape (start delays up to 2.67 ms, 95–100% misses), but the A100 host was noisy.

## C2: stream priority does nothing across processes (M1 == M0)

- **[M] This is expected from how the GPU schedules, so it is not a finding.**
  - The GPU takes turns between processes in fixed slices. Stream priority only orders work within the process that currently has the GPU (Bakita & Anderson describe this).
  - NVIDIA documents no priority between processes. NVIDIA forum guidance says streams in different processes are unrelated.
- **[L] The test itself is fair.** The driver runs at −5 (`meta.json`) and the AI job at 0. On this GPU "low" = 0 = default (`adversary.cu:616`), so only the driver's priority changes.
- **Could it reverse?** No. Narrow it to "across processes without MPS".
- **Fix:** test priority where it can work:
  - the AI job as a low-priority stream inside the driver process;
  - M2 with the driver at default priority;
  - `CUDA_MPS_CLIENT_PRIORITY=1` for the AI job.

## C3: MPS restores start timing (4–6 µs) but slows execution; protects vs LLM/vision, not sgemm

- **[H] 4–6 µs is below what the clock mapping can resolve.**
  - Start delays go negative, which is physically impossible: down to −17.8 µs on the 3060 and −88 µs on the A100.
  - Under M2, median start delay is 1.4 µs (vision) and 2.1 µs (sgemm), lower than idle (3.5–4.8 µs), which is not plausible.
  - The conclusion survives if stated without the mapping: latency minus execution time is 8–14 µs on the worst M2 slots. Report it as "same as idle, within ~10 µs".
- **[H] Mixed causes in M2.**
  - M2 changes three things at once (`run_one.sh:95,109`): the driver keeps high priority, MPS is on, and only the AI job is capped at 50%. That is not a partition: the slot can still land on the AI job's SMs.
  - "Slower because of shared SMs" overstates it:
    - MPS with an idle neighbour already adds 7% (247.8 vs 231.4 µs);
    - throttling adds more (M2 sgemm SM clock 1830 MHz);
    - contention is the remainder (llm 356, vision 345, sgemm 435 µs median).
  - **Fix:** a factorial run:
    - MPS with no cap, and caps of 25/50/75%;
    - driver priority default vs high;
    - a cap on the driver as well;
    - client priority;
    - green contexts.
- **[H] "Protects vs LLM/vision" (generalisation).**
  - Worst-case latency (99.99th percentile) is 398.7 µs (LLM) and 407.8 µs (vision), against a 500 µs deadline, with a slot sized at ~231 µs idle.
  - Execution grows about 1.5× under MPS, so protection fails once the idle slot exceeds about 320 µs.
  - Only proxies were tested; the real-LLM runs failed.
  - **Fixes:**
    - sweep the slot size (`--tune-us` 150–350) and report the largest slot MPS still protects;
    - real llama.cpp/vLLM and YOLO;
    - a batched-serving AI job, which behaves like sgemm.
- **[M] The start timestamp is a 1-thread kernel that fits on any SM** (`phy_kernels.cu:24-33`, `slot_pipeline.cu:294`). Under MPS, later kernels that wait for SM space get counted as "execution", so "starts on time" is partly true by construction. **Fix:** timestamps at each stage, e.g. extra timestamp kernels around the LDPC decode.
- **[M] The A100's 0.4% is mixed up with host noise.**
  - A100 idle runs miss 0.036–1.1% (M0 idle: 1127 of 100604).
  - In M2 llm, only 10 of the 87 late slots had execution > 500 µs. There were 310 skipped boundaries, and CPU wake-up was up to 5.0 ms late.
  - **Fix:** split misses by cause using the wake / start-lateness / launch-cost / execution times per slot, or rerun on isolated cores.

## C4: CUDA graph vs individual stream launches makes no difference (M4 == M1)

- **[H] Both conditions are near 100% misses, and the claim is wrong about mechanism.**
  - At 50% duty, M4 is measurably worse. M4 / M1 / M5:
    - llm: 0.5448 / 0.5402 / 0.5406
    - sgemm: 0.6392 / 0.6325 / 0.6333
  - The difference exceeds the M1-vs-M5 repeat spread.
  - In streams mode, the GPU queue runs dry between host launches and the slot gets sliced out mid-execution: 152 of the worst 200 slots in M4 llm 50% have execution ≈ 2.50 ms. sgemm execution peaks at 2.55 ms. Graph mode never shows this.
  - Idle cost: +12 µs (243.7 vs 231.4 µs).
- **Could it reverse?** Yes. The finding becomes "graphs protect against being sliced out mid-slot".
- **Fix:** run M4 under MPS and when idle, and report the execution-time tails.

## C5: same behaviour; under MPS the A100 keeps ~94% of LLM throughput vs ~64% on the 3060

- **[H] Different slot work.** `--tune-us` picked 4 LDPC iterations on the A100 and 5 on the 3060 (`meta.json` `config.phy.ldpc_iters`).
- **[H] The slot is a different share of each GPU.**
  - The decoder runs 22 blocks (`ldpc_cb`=22). That is 22 of 28 SMs on the 3060 and 22 of 108 on the A100.
  - With the AI job capped at 50%, the A100 leaves 54 free SMs (≥ 22); the 3060 leaves 14 (< 22).
  - LLM vector-matrix work saturates HBM with 54 SMs, while 14 SMs may not saturate GDDR6.
  - So 94% vs 64% mostly reflects "slot size relative to GPU" plus the cap, not consumer vs datacenter.
  - **Fix:**
    - make the slot the same fraction of each GPU (e.g. 100 MHz or several cells on the A100);
    - run an AI-only baseline under MPS at 50%;
    - sweep the cap.
- **[M] Throughput is diluted unevenly.**
  - Throughput counts the AI job's whole life, including settle time with no slot running (`run_one.sh:399-401`). The slot was running for 82% of that time on the 3060 (settle 10 s) but only 61% on the A100 (`proof.toml` settle 30 s).
  - Corrected bounds: 56–64% (3060) vs 91–94% (A100). The direction survives; the size is uncertain.
  - **Fix:** count AI units only inside the driver's run window, and ship `adversary_timeline.csv`.
- **[M] Other differences between the two machines:**
  - "slightly more misses" is about 100× (0.4% vs ≤5e-5) and is mostly host noise;
  - different driver versions (13000 vs 13020);
  - AI-only baseline 30 s vs 60 s;
  - one rented machine of each.

## C6: CPU and GPU clocks agree to ±1.2 µs; drift < 1.6 ppm

- **[H] Not supported as stated.**
  - **±1.2 µs is only at calibration time.** It is essentially the GPU timer's tick: all execution times are multiples of 1.024 µs, e.g. 257.024 = 251 × 1.024. On the A100 the bound is 2.2–2.8 µs.
  - **The clock rate depends on load.** Average rate over a run:
    - 3060 idle/llm: −1.0 to −1.4 ppm
    - 3060 sgemm 100%: −2.1 to −3.0 ppm
    - 3060 vision 100%: −2.5 to −2.9 ppm
    - A100: +7.4 to +9.3 ppm
  - The rate measured before and after a run differs by up to 9.9 ppm. Each calibration window lasts only about 0.14 s.
  - **Mid-run error is not bounded.** The impossible negative start delays mean the mapping was off by at least 18 µs on the 3060 and 88 µs on the A100 at some point in a run (`stats.py:208-217` interpolates straight between the before and after calibrations).
- **Fixes:**
  - run `clockcal` for hours, idle and under load, logging temperature;
  - recalibrate during the run, or track the clock offset from the minimum of (t1 − g1) over a moving window;
  - add a validation check that flags negative start delays;
  - make latency minus execution time the main breakdown metric.

## C7: contribution (start-late vs ran-slow breakdown; worst-case tails; consumer vs datacenter)

- **[M] Novelty.**
  - Logging kernel start and end with the GPU timer is standard in real-time GPU research (Otterness et al.'s cuda_scheduling_examiner; Bakita & Anderson's exec_logger).
  - Only the RAN framing is new, and `related_work.md:3` admits only YinYangRAN was read in full. Weaver (Sep 2026) is unchecked.
- **[M] The breakdown only has two points (first and last kernel).** It cannot separate "waited for SMs" from "ran slowly", or throttling from contention. Being sliced out mid-slot in streams mode counts as execution.
- **[M] "Worst-case tails over many slots" is thin.**
  - Each run is 100k slots (~50 s), done once. DESIGN §8 asks for 1e6 slots and three repeats.
  - The 99.99th percentile is just the 10th-largest sample.
  - The GPU was not at a steady temperature: 65–79 °C, and run lengths range from 51 s (MPS) to 250 s.
  - Present M5 as a repeat of M1, not as a mechanism (clock locking was refused).

# Prior-art check for slotbench claims C1–C7 (as of 2026-10-01)

**Bottom line:** most of the mechanisms behind C1–C4, C6 and C7 are already in the general GPU real-time literature or in NVIDIA's documentation. What prior work has not reported is these effects measured per slot in an AI+RAN setting, the dependence of MPS protection on which AI neighbour runs, and an AI+RAN study on a consumer GPU. Several claims also need rewording to match the data (details under "Things to fix").

## C1. Co-located AI makes the slot start ~2 ms late through time-slicing; execution unchanged
**Rating: the mechanism is known in general GPU work. That shared GPUs break RAN deadlines is known for RAN. The specific cause (start delay of about one time slice, execution roughly unchanged) is new for RAN.**
- Gilman & Walls, *Characterizing Concurrency Mechanisms for NVIDIA GPUs under Deep Learning Workloads*, Performance Evaluation 2021, measured on an RTX 3090, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00459
  - "Empirically, we determined that the time slice length is fixed to approximately 2ms."
  - "time slices were fixed-length and assigned round-robin across processes"
  - "two kernels from separate processes are never executed on the GPU at the same time"
- Bakita & Anderson, *Demystifying NVIDIA GPU Internals…*, RTAS 2024, measured on a GTX 1060, https://www.cs.unc.edu/~jbakita/rtas24.pdf
  - "The slice lengths are largely consistent, at about 2 ms each."
  - "Only one task executes instructions at any given time, despite each task requiring only a fraction of the GPU."
- NVIDIA MPS documentation (Release 615), https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/pdf/CUDA_Multi_Process_Service_Overview.pdf
  - "The GPU also has a time sliced scheduler to schedule work from work queues belonging to different CUDA contexts. Work launched to the compute engine from work queues belonging to different CUDA contexts cannot execute concurrently."
  - "each MPI process is assigned a serially scheduled time-slice on the whole GPU."
- NVIDIA vGPU scheduling docs, https://docs.nvidia.com/vgpu/20.0/grid-vgpu-user-guide/changing-vgpu-scheduling-policy.html: the default frequency is "480 Hz" with fewer than 8 vGPUs, which is about 2.08 ms per slice.
- Wang et al., GCAPS, ECRTS 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05221. It models this case directly: "a TSG of a task has to wait for the completion of the preceding other tasks' TSGs before it starts". Its delay bound is (L+θ)·ν·⌈G/L⌉. On Tegra the default slice is 1024 µs.
- Olmedo et al., RTAS 2020, https://conferences.computer.org/cpsiot/pdfs/RTAS2020-4uXAu5nqG7QNiz5wFYyfj6/549900a210/549900a210.pdf, describe the scheduler as "work-conserving TDMA". This explains why the vision cells wait only ~1.18 ms rather than 2 ms: the slice ends early when the adversary goes idle.
- For RAN, YinYangRAN (INFOCOM 2024, A100), https://neclab.eu/fileadmin/user_upload/YinYangRAN_Resource_Multiplexing_in_GPU-Accelerated_Virtualized_RANs_pre-print.pdf
  - Naive co-location "results in substantial disruption… a low 50% probability of meeting latency targets".
  - It blames "time-sliced context switching" overhead. It never separates waiting from execution.

## C2. Stream priority does nothing across processes (M1 == M0)
**Rating: known in general GPU work. Not reported for RAN, so it only confirms known behaviour.**
- Amert et al., *GPU Scheduling on the NVIDIA TX2: Hidden Details Revealed*, RTSS 2017, https://www.cs.unc.edu/~anderson/papers/rtss17c.pdf
  - "We found that assigning priorities to the streams of different processes had no effect… the usage of stream priorities has impact only in the context of task-based computing, not process-based computing."
- GCAPS (ECRTS 2024): "the effect of stream scheduling and stream priority assignment is exerted only within each process boundary."
- Gilman 2021, Table 2: priority streams require that "All applications must be launched from within the same process."
- **Nuance:** under MPS, the driver does compare stream priorities across clients. The MPS doc says that without client-priority control, "the streams from Process B would be executed first by the CUDA driver". M2 is MPS plus a high-priority slot stream, so it mixes two mechanisms.

## C3. MPS restores start sync but slows execution; it protects against LLM and vision proxies but not back-to-back sgemm
**Rating: the core trade-off is known for RAN. That the outcome depends on which neighbour runs is known in general GPU work but new for RAN.**
- For RAN:
  - YinYangRAN builds its controller on the MPS SM-split trade-off. It notes that under MPS "cache and memory resources are still shared… large overheads when memory-intensive processes compete".
  - Aerial ISAC (arXiv 2512.06493) runs MPS without MIG and attributes the slowdown to "GPU memory subsystem contention", with MIG about 1.8x better for the AI side.
  - Aerial cuPHY itself partitions its channels with MPS SM upper bounds.
- In general GPU work:
  - Gilman O5: "MPS… caused intra-SM resource contention that added to the execution times".
  - Villarrubia et al., arXiv 2604.22430 (2026): "co-execution with MPS can yield extreme outcomes, both positive and negative".
  - The CUDA guide on green contexts (https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/green-contexts.html) says MPS SMs "can be any N SMs… vary over time". It also says "even with higher stream priority, the critical kernel can start executing as soon as some thread blocks complete". That is the likely reason sgemm defeats MPS at a 50% cap.
- **What is new:** no RAN paper compares neighbour types under MPS. The finding that a compute-bound GEMM is the harmful neighbour, rather than the memory-bound LLM proxy, also runs against the RAN papers' emphasis on memory contention. It also contrasts with Weaver's premise that GEMM training complements memory-bound RAN work, although Weaver uses hard green-context partitions.

## C4. CUDA graphs vs individual stream launches makes no difference under contention (M4 == M1)
**Rating: apparently new as a measurement, but low value because the known mechanism predicts it.**
- NVIDIA documents graphs only as a launch-overhead saving:
  - cuPHY "makes use of CUDA graph feature to reduce kernel launch latency".
  - RANPerf: graph mode "yields lower latency and lower CPU overhead".
- Time slices are scheduled per context (TSG), so how the work was submitted should not matter (Bakita 2024).
- No paper tests graphs vs streams under cross-process contention. The data matches the documented benefit: graphs save about 12 µs when idle (231 vs 244 µs).

## C5. Consumer vs datacenter: same qualitative behaviour; A100 keeps ~94% of LLM-proxy throughput under MPS vs ~64% on the 3060
**Rating: apparently new for RAN.**
- Consumer GPUs appear in general sharing studies: Gilman (RTX 3090), Bakita (GTX 1060 up to RTX 6000 Ada), Nixie arXiv 2601.11743 (RTX 4090/5090).
- In RAN they appear only for LDPC with no neighbour:
  - Barker et al., arXiv 2602.04652 (2026), RTX 4090 vs DGX Spark. Its future-work list is the gap the student fills: "Future work should measure p95/p99/p99.99 decode latency and end-to-end slot-budget composition under contention… and concurrent edge workloads."
  - Li et al. (Titan RTX).
  - Jang & Kim, *Empirical Tail-Latency Characterization of GPU-Accelerated 5G NR LDPC Decoding*, MDPI Telecom 7(5):120, Sep 2026: a "single dynamically clocked WSL2 GPU", LDPC only.
- Every AI+RAN co-location study I found uses A100, GH200, DGX Spark or L40S.
- **My guess, not checked:** the 94% vs 64% gap may come from a 50% thread cap on 108 SMs vs 28 SMs, and how much bandwidth each can saturate, rather than consumer vs datacenter as such.

## C6. CPU and GPU clocks agree to about ±1.2 µs; drift < ~1.6 ppm
**Rating: known in general GPU work, and not a contribution.**
- On an NVIDIA forum, Robert Crovella (2024) quotes: "The default resolution is 32ns with update every µs. The NVIDIA performance tools force the update to every 32 ns", https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/questions-about-globaltimer-functionality-accessing-and-configuring/304268
- The data shows the same thing:
  - Every gpu_exec value is an exact multiple of 1.024 µs, on both GPUs.
  - The fit residual rms is 290–360 ns, about 1.024/√12 µs = 296 ns.
  - So ±1.2 µs is the timer's tick size, not a property of the clocks.
- CUPTI and Nsight already correlate CPU and GPU timestamps.
- **The data does not support the claimed numbers:**
  - On the A100, eps is 2.2–2.8 µs, not 1.2.
  - Rate estimates per cell range from −8.5 to +12 ppm, and the difference between pre-run and post-run fits reaches about 10 ppm.

## C7. Contribution: splitting each miss into "GPU started late" vs "GPU ran slow"; worst-case tails; consumer vs datacenter
**Rating: the concept is known in general GPU work and a close analogue exists for RAN. It is new for RAN only in the specific form of per-slot GPU timestamps with an AI co-tenant.**
- The concept is standard:
  - NVIDIA Nsight blog (2020): launch latency is "the time range from the beginning of the launch API call to the beginning of the kernel execution", https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/understanding-the-visualization-of-overhead-and-latency-in-nsight-systems/
  - CUPTI kernel records carry queued, submitted, start and end timestamps.
  - NVIDIA's HoloHub green-context benchmark (Oct 2025) measures exactly "launch-start time" under background load, within one process, on RTX 6000 Ada and Orin: https://nvidia-holoscan.github.io/holohub/benchmarks/green_context_benchmarking/
  - eunomia.dev (2026-08-20) uses the same framing: "Was the GPU Kernel Slow, or Did It Just Start Late?"
- RAN analogues:
  - CloudRIC (MobiCom 2024) admits work only if waiting w + processing d < D − δ, modelling processing latency "without queuing delays". Its waiting time is a software queue in front of shared accelerators.
  - Weaver (arXiv 2609.35276) counts an "isolation violation… when a training kernel still occupies SMs required by the RAN at decoding start".
- Tails as such are not new: Concordia, Nuberu and CloudRIC target 99.999%. Each cell here has only 100k slots, so p99.99 rests on about 10 slots.

## What is actually new
1. In an AI+RAN setting, per-slot evidence that a miss is a GPU-side start delay of about one time slice of the co-tenant (~2 ms; ~1.2 ms for a bursty vision neighbour), with execution close to idle, on both an RTX 3060 and an A100. The mechanism is published; the attribution in RAN is not.
2. MPS protection depends on the neighbour: a back-to-back GEMM defeats a 50% MPS cap (15–33% misses on the 3060, 18% on the A100), while the LLM and vision proxies do not.
3. The first AI+RAN co-location results on a consumer GPU.
4. C2, C4 and unlocked clocks (M5) are useful negative results for RAN, but they are confirmations of known behaviour, not contributions.
5. The open harness covering several sharing mechanisms.

## Things to fix before writing up
- **C1:**
  - "Execution unchanged" is too strong. With sgemm, execution rises by 13% on the 3060 (231 to 261 µs) and by 5% on the A100. Context-switch cache pollution and flushing is the documented explanation (GCAPS; Villarrubia).
  - "~2 ms" does not hold for the vision cells, which wait about 1.18 ms.
- **C3/C7:** the start stamp S0 is a 1-thread kernel. Under MPS it can start on any free SM while the large kernels wait for SMs held by sgemm blocks. That waiting for SMs is then counted as "ran slow", so the split understates the start delay.
- **M2** combines MPS with a high-priority slot stream. MPS's static SM partitioning (`-S`, Ampere and newer) and green contexts are the hard-partition options that were not tested.

## Audit: relevance

## Industry relevance: does anything we found matter to practitioners?

### Bottom line
Yes, one result matters a lot and two matter as warnings.

- **C3 matters most.** MPS keeps the slot on time next to inference-style neighbours but not next to a back-to-back GEMM neighbour. MPS is what NVIDIA's own L1 uses inside its process. It is also the only spatial-sharing option on the GPUs aimed at cell sites, which have no MIG or only 2-way MIG. And the AI-RAN business case sells spare capacity for training and paid AI jobs, which is GEMM-heavy work.
- **C1 and C2 are useful warnings.** They show what goes wrong when you share without MPS or MIG, or rely on stream priority. But no vendor deploys that way on purpose.
- **C4 and C6 matter little to industry.** C5 and C7 matter only if reframed (below).
- **The biggest gap:** the industry default is MIG, and we have no MIG data (M6 was never run). None of the public sources publish L1 slot-tail numbers under MIG either.

Only the idle saving (~12 us per slot) is visible, and it is already known. It would matter only if run under MPS. |
| **C5** consumer and datacenter GPUs behave the same | **5** if reframed | More relevant than we framed it: the industry's cell-site GPU is now a GDDR, near-consumer chip (ARC-Pro's GB203). But the 3060 is not a stand-in for ARC-Pro. The useful comparison is memory bandwidth and MIG availability: 94% vs 64% LLM-proxy throughput fits HBM against GDDR6 for a memory-bound neighbour. Evidence is weak: single runs, 4 vs 5 LDPC iterations on the two cards, and the A100 host's CPU wake-up overshoot reached 4.6 ms. |
| **C6** CPU and GPU clocks agree to ~±1.2 us | **3** | This validates our measurement method; it is not an industry result. Operators care about PTP at the NIC and radio unit: ±1.5 us at the air interface ([Ericsson](https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/ericsson-technology-review/articles/5g-synchronization-requirements-and-solutions)). Aerial uses the GPU clock only for relative timeouts. |
| **C7** splitting each miss into "started late" vs "ran slow" | **6** | Not done in Weaver or Aerial ISAC (checked), not in SoftBank's material, and NVIDIA's RANPerf measures execution only. Aerial's real failure messages ("Order kernel timeout", "PUSCH Pre Early Harq Wait kernel timeout") are start-side failures, so the split is a useful diagnostic. Two weaknesses: "the CPU asks, the GPU starts" does not describe Aerial's data-driven pipeline (better: data ready → first SM busy), and 100k slots is far from carrier-grade evidence. |

### What would make this matter more (in priority order)
1. Run M6 MIG on the A100, with the same GEMM and LLM neighbours. That is the industry baseline, and no slot-tail data under MIG has been published.
2. Re-score every cell against Aerial-style budgets (PUSCH 1200 us, slots overlapping), keeping the 500 us result alongside.
3. Cap the RAN's SMs as Aerial does, and run MPS with and without priority to separate the two effects.
4. Present C5 as "GDDR without MIG vs HBM with MIG" rather than "consumer vs datacenter".
5. Run more slots per cell, and on a host with locked clocks and an RT kernel where possible.

Green contexts are low priority: they work only inside one process, so they cannot isolate a separate AI pod, and Aerial still marks them experimental.
